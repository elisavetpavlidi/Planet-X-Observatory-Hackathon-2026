EUREKA / OPEN ASTRONOMY

Explore the sky.
Contribute to discovery.

EUREKA is an open astronomy platform for everyone. Investigate real sky observations, flag unexplained apparent motion, and document evidence that scientists could investigate further.

Search observations from SPHEREx and NEOWISE. When suitable repeated images exist, examine potential moving sources, check the evidence, and record unanswered questions. A source that has not been identified is not automatically a new object.

Our goal is a citizen-to-researcher discovery workflow: public observations, independent checks, and expert investigation. This version is a local prototype, not a live scientific submission service.

No scientific background needed. Just curiosity.

QUICK GUIDE

From sky to source.

  1. Choose a place. Use the sky map, an object name, or RA / Dec coordinates.
  2. Choose dates and archive. Find observations. The list keeps all returned exposures for your filters.
  3. Compare images. Play a group of up to 12 consecutive images. Use Earlier / Later to continue, or select images in the list.
  4. Select what moves. Choose a suggestion, or mark the same source in successive frames. Your source shows its measured shift, rate and motion assessment.
  5. Check identity. Compare the measured positions with a possible known Solar System object.
  6. Save your work. Save session includes the loaded images and marks. Open it again without downloading them.

Recommended observations work offline. New searches and new identity checks need an internet connection.

DATA & COVERAGE

Real images. Available dates.

Explore any sky coordinate and choose any date range. Images exist only where and when a supported mission observed. An empty result means no returned exposures for those filters.

WISE / NEOWISE: public W1 and W2 single exposures from the All-Sky, 3-band, Post-Cryo and Reactivation releases, 2010–2011 and 2013–2024. SPHEREx: public QR2 / QR3 Wide and Deep collections from 2025, subject to each release's actual coverage and service availability. This app does not connect to every NASA mission.

Long searches run in year-sized windows. Every returned metadata row is retained. Images load in groups of up to 12 to keep memory bounded; no time sampling removes exposures. The list reports incomplete searches and failed image downloads.

Images use WCS alignment, official masks, and a common display stretch. Blank pixels are missing or excluded data. SPHEREx brightness depends on wavelength; incompatible bands disable automatic tracks. The saved SPHEREx observations are a static M31 quality comparison.

Automatic tracks are exploratory: at least three independent epochs within 48 hours and stationary control sources. Galaxies normally provide a stationary background here. This app primarily helps inspect asteroids and comets; a bright residual alone is not motion.

IMCCE SkyBoT preselects up to three nearby objects. NASA/JPL Horizons checks positions at the observation times from WISE or SPHEREx. The geocentric preselection can miss close objects. A match is a candidate, and no match does not establish a discovery.
